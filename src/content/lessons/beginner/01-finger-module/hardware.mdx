The Mega is the board that runs your program. The motor shield sits on top of it and switches the motor on and off. Unplug the battery and the USB cable before you move any wire.

<Callout type="safety">
A moving link can pinch. Keep your fingers off the joints while the battery is connected. If a part feels hot or the finger stops halfway, unplug the battery first.
</Callout>

## Connections

Use these connections. The motor has two wires. The button has two legs.

| Part | Connect it to |
| --- | --- |
| Motor shield | Press it onto the Mega so every pin lines up |
| The motor's two wires | The shield's terminal marked M1, one wire in each hole |
| Battery, red wire | The + side of the shield's power terminal |
| Battery, black wire | The − side of the shield's power terminal |
| One button leg | Pin 2 |
| Other button leg | GND |

The shield has its own row of holes above the Mega's pins. Pin 2 and GND are reached through those.

Pin 2 listens to the button. GND is the shared ground. The button does not need a resistor, because the program turns on the Mega's built-in pull-up.

## Power

The motor runs from the battery, not from the Mega. The USB cable powers the Mega and carries your program to it.

Plug in the USB cable and the battery only after every wire is in the right hole.

Wiring: 25 min

<TeacherOnly title="Before class">
The shield talks to the Mega through the pins it is pressed onto, so it has to be seated all the way down. With the Mega on USB and the battery on the shield's power terminal, Adafruit's guide for this shield says to leave its small power jumper off.
</TeacherOnly>
<TeacherOnly title="If the motor does not turn">
Check the battery first, then that both motor wires are held in M1. If the finger opens when it should close, swap the motor's two wires.
</TeacherOnly>
